# Chapter 8 — Profile Control

## 8.1 What a good profile looks like

The ideal word-line trench has near-vertical sidewalls (88–89°), a slightly rounded floor in silicon, a smooth transition at the fin shoulder, no bow below the mask and no notch at the foot. The sidewall becomes the channel surface, so its roughness directly scatters carriers and its crystal damage degrades gate-oxide quality.

## 8.2 Taper

**Taper** — walls that lean inward with depth — arises when passivation deposits faster than ions remove it. More O₂, higher wall-film sticking, or lower bias all increase taper. Mild taper is useful: it narrows the floor, rounds the bottom and makes subsequent fill easier. Excessive taper shortens the effective gate width and pinches the trench before target depth.

## 8.3 Bowing

**Bowing** is an outward bulge, usually 20–50 nm below the mask. Chapter 5 showed that ions which miss the floor strike the walls in the upper part of the trench. Ions scattered off the mask facet at grazing angles add to this. Where that wall flux exceeds what the passivation can absorb, the wall etches laterally. Remedies: tighter ion angular distribution, more passivation early, a less faceted hard mask.

## 8.4 Floor shape

A flat floor with sharp corners concentrates the electric field at the corners of the finished transistor and degrades reliability. A **rounded** floor is preferred. Rounding comes from a slightly broad ion distribution near the floor, from a short isotropic fluorine step, or from oxidation and strip after etch.

## 8.5 The fin shoulder

Where the trench crosses from silicon into oxide, the silicon side of the fin is exposed below the silicon floor. Its shape decides how well the gate wraps the fin. Sharp shoulders give field crowding and gate-oxide thinning; very rounded shoulders reduce effective width. The shoulder is shaped by the over-etch step and by subsequent cleans.

## 8.6 Line-edge roughness and wiggling

Roughness transferred from the mask becomes sidewall roughness. In a 20 nm trench, ±1.5 nm of roughness is 15 % of the width. Line wiggling — low-frequency lateral wander — has less effect on the transistor than on the later contact landing, where a wandering word line narrows the space for the bit-line contact.

## 8.7 Summary table

| Defect | Main cause | First lever |
|---|---|---|
| Taper too strong | excess passivation | less O₂, higher bias |
| Bowing | wall ion flux, mask facet | tighter σ, early passivation |
| Square floor | anisotropic finish | rounding step |
| Notch at fin foot | oxide charging | pulsed bias, lower AR at landing |
| Rough wall | mask LER | mask smoothing, polymer trim |
